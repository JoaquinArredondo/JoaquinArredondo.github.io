01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

How do you make marine predators feel alive without losing gameplay clarity?

VR Ocean Site One is an immersive experience designed to build biodiversity awareness through interaction around a Santa Barbara offshore environment. The featured mini-game asks the player to navigate the scene while engaging with marine life and an egg-chasing objective.

I contributed two critical predator assets—a shark and a barracuda—and carried them from Maya into Unity. My work included modeling, rigging, weight painting, animation, integration, animation-controller setup, AI-behavior support, testing, and iteration with the broader team.

04 / ANIMATION & AI

Animation became part of the gameplay system—not a layer added at the end.

In Unity, the predator animations had to respond to gameplay state. I connected animation controllers, transitions, and events to the fish-behavior implementation so movement could communicate intent and support the player experience.

The goal was not simply to make each fish swim. It was to create a reliable handoff between AI logic and animation, with clips and state changes that remained understandable to teammates working in the larger project.

06 / RESULTS & REFLECTION

A technical-art project shaped by the complete production loop.

OSO strengthened my ability to move between art tools, engine systems, gameplay needs, and team communication. The most useful lesson was that an asset is not complete when it looks good—it is complete when it behaves reliably inside the experience.

01

Completed two animated predator assets and integrated them into a Unity XR experience.

WHAT WORKED

Owning the full asset path.

Controlling the model, rig, animation, and integration made it easier to diagnose problems without treating each stage as a separate black box.

WHAT I LEARNED

VR changes the standard for readability.

Scale, motion, timing, and player comfort must be judged from the headset. A movement that reads on a monitor may feel unclear or overwhelming when it surrounds the player.

NEXT ITERATION

Measure and tune behavior more deliberately.

A future pass would formalize animation-state documentation, profile runtime performance, and compare player responses across multiple predator timing and distance settings.
